Blogs in depth: starting an article, every AI writer question and option, the editor's tabs and sections, looks (and the Classic/Luxe naming), AI cover, fact-check, publishing and scheduling, Google and share settings, and where articles show.

## Who gets it
- Premium, IDX and No-MLS Premium. Locked on Basic and No-MLS Basic.
- **Luxe** article look: IDX and No-MLS Premium.
- **Show on my IDX Site**: IDX only ("Upgrade to IDX to publish your blogs on your own IDX website too").
- What the AI writer draws on depends on your plan: MLS plans - "Your sales, live Bright MLS numbers and verified research on your towns"; No-MLS Premium - "What you told us, plus verified research on the towns you picked".

## Naming you must get right
The looks are **Classic**, **Luxe**, **Editorial**, **Modern**, **Midnight**:
- **Classic** - "Ivory and gold with serif headlines - the standard look" (the default).
- **Luxe** - "Cinematic cover, gold light and motion - the full OpenHousePal Luxe" (IDX / No-MLS Premium), with a **Luxe finish** metal.
- **Editorial** - black-and-white magazine page, big serif type.
- **Modern** - clean, crisp sans-serif with a gold accent.
- **Midnight** - dark and dramatic with warm gold highlights.
Classic has a **Classic color** picker for its accent.

## The Blogs page (Tools > Blogs)
- **Create a Blog** / **Create**. Search ("Search articles..."), status filter, and **Reads** per article.
- Each card: status (Draft, Scheduled, Published, Unpublished, Archived), **Share** (copy link), **Shows on** switches **Agent Hub** and **IDX Site**, delete ("Delete this article?").
- AI drafts in progress show "AI is writing"; failed ones show "AI couldn't finish" / "Unfinished AI draft" with **Try again** / **Finish with AI**.

## Starting an article ("New Article")
1. **What's it about?** - **My own idea** ("Type any topic - a neighborhood, a market question, a story from a closing") or tap an idea. Then **Give it a title** ("A clear, specific title works best - the AI builds the whole article around it").
2. **How should we write it?**
   - **Write it with AI** - "A few quick questions about the area and your take, then a complete article in your voice." Uses your monthly AI allowance.
   - **Start from scratch** - opens with a suggested structure for that kind of article, or a blank article - you write it.

## The AI writer, question by question
- **What angle should it take?** / **What's this article about?** - a sentence is plenty; tap an angle built from your title.
- **What should a reader walk away knowing - or doing?** (optional, sharpens the article).
- **Who is it for?** (pick any): Buyers, Sellers, First-time buyers, Move-up buyers, Luxury buyers, Investors, Relocating, Homeowners.
- **What area are you writing about?** - up to 5 towns or neighborhoods. "Naming them is what makes it rank locally." A switch adds a Town Spotlight for each (a photo, live market numbers, schools and what's nearby - verified and dated). A town not covered yet takes a minute or two the first time.
- **Key points to cover** - bullets are fine.
- **Your take, or a local story only you could tell** - "what makes it yours instead of generic".
- **Numbers you want cited** - "only what you know is true. The AI never invents a statistic."
- **Reference your real sales or listings?** (optional) - pick up to 4 of **Your sales**; **Your active listings** are added as property cards.
- **Tie in one of your videos?** (optional) - an Agent Hub video or a pasted YouTube link; **Where it goes**: **Its own section** (the AI writes about it), **After the intro**, or **Near the end**.
- **Tone**: **Warm & personal** (trusted neighbor), **Professional** (clear, authoritative), **Refined luxury** (editorial, understated), **Straight talk** (plain, zero fluff), **Energetic** (upbeat).
- **Length**: **Quick read** (~650 words, 3 min), **Standard** (~1,000 words, 5 min), **In-depth guide** (~1,700 words, 8 min).
- **What should readers do at the end?** - the closing button opens a form right on the article: Ready to Buy or Sell?, What's My Home Worth?, Schedule a Showing, I'm Buying, I'm Selling, Make Me Sell, or Ask Me a Question.
- Then **Write my article**.

## From the AI helper chat
- "Write a blog post about ..." (or "suggest a topic" - the ideas come as buttons to tap) sets up a card with the Blogs page's own interview as check-box steps, each folding when they tap its **Done**: **Topic** (the angle - what they asked for or a sharper title - and what readers walk away with), **Readers** (who it's for, towns up to 5, add a town, **Add a Town Spotlight**), **Key points** (ideas to check off, their own point, their take, numbers to cite), **Your homes** (their sales - the ones in the picked towns first, up to 4 - and active listings as home cards), **Video** (their YouTube videos, the ones that fit the article first, or paste a link - the AI writes a section around it) and **Style** (Tone, Length with about how much of the monthly AI each uses - a full researched article is usually 3-6% - and what readers tap at the end). **Write it** starts it; the card shows it working, and when the draft is ready a message with **Open the draft** posts in that chat (the chat button shows a red number), even if they closed the site. It's always a draft - nothing goes live until they publish.

## While it writes
- Stages: "Gathering what we know about your towns", "Researching what is missing" (official sources only - county, school districts, Freddie Mac, the state), "Writing in your voice", "Fact-checking every claim" (numbers, dates, geography, Fair Housing, anything about your experience), "Fixing and finishing".
- "Usually 2-4 minutes." **Keep working - tell me when it's done**: it keeps writing, your bell lights up, and it waits in your Blogs list.
- "A try that fails is never charged." Your answers are saved as a draft.
- Who it's written for: the agent's clients and everyday readers (buyers, sellers, neighbors) - never other agents. Plain words, with any real estate term (days on market, seller assist, contingency, millage) explained the first time; a price or market article leads with each town's dated numbers (median sale price, how fast homes sold, homes for sale) and ends with a short "bottom line" before the call to action. Articles about a whole region ("the Main Line", "Philly suburbs") use each of the agent's towns' live numbers.
- It's always a finished article: it never leaves blanks or "fill in your numbers" placeholders, and a heading never sits empty. If anything like that slips through, it's removed before the draft is saved (the Verified report lists it under **Unfinished text**), and the published page hides it too. **Too technical for readers** marks wording the fact-check simplified.
- Each town's numbers also say what kinds of homes are for sale (condos and townhomes vs single-family houses, one-level homes, 55+ communities), what each kind sold for, the condo and townhome fees (all fees combined) and what they usually cover, and use one tax year per town. Articles about towns in another state (MD, VA, DC, DE, NJ) use that state's taxes, programs and rules. A downsizing article also covers avoiding a double move (rent-back, bridge loan), smaller-home options with each town's live counts, and tax relief for older homeowners. If the agent's video lands in a section that doesn't mention it, a short "watch ... just below" line is added above it.
- Laws, deadlines, assistance programs, tax breaks, exemptions and loan limits are only NAMED (what they do, in general words, "conditions apply") - never their amounts, income limits, day counts or deadlines, since those change and nobody re-checks a published article. Readers are sent to the agent, and a small **Ask me about ...** line (first person, with the agent's photo) sits right under that sentence with **Call**, **Text**, **Email** and **Message** (Message opens the article's contact form). At most 3 per article. Agents can add one by hand: Add section > Action > **Ask Me line (call · text · email)**, with an optional topic. The Verified report lists **Rules and programs this article names** to check before publishing, and **Fine print taken out** when the fact-check removed a detail.
- The writer never decides whether a law, tax break or disclosure duty applies to a reader's situation: it explains the general idea, says exceptions apply (naming the one that fits, like estate sales), and points to the right professional - always in the agent's own first-person voice, never "ask [name]". The fact-check flags misses as **Depends on the reader**. Inherited-home articles get their own checklist (who can sell, estate disclosure rules, heirs' tax credits, the general tax-basis rule).
- Towns outside every state the agent works in: the article never claims to live, work or have sold homes there, and keeps the agent's chosen closing button and first-person closing (e.g. "I'll start your search" - the agent may hand it to a referral agent); the Verified report says **Outside your area**.
- Every article also gets plain-code checks in its one run (no second version): a built-in list of the state rules and programs its reader should hear about (first-time buyers, condo/HOA buyers, buyers and sellers, older owners - e.g. Maryland's first-time-buyer closing-tax rules, condo resale documents and cancel rights), each named only and linked to the official law; if the writer leaves one out, it's added (Verified report: **Reader protection**). Tax rates, mills, program limits and legal deadlines, "half sold above asking" readings of a median, townhomes treated like condos and personal stories the agent didn't give (their own "personal take" is kept) are sent back for one fix, and anything left is cut before saving.
- Its last step is a built-in grade against a fixed **pass list**: no wrong or made-up facts, right place (towns, counties, school districts), numbers say what they cover, no fine print, numbers read correctly, no invented stories, reader protections, fits the reader, the agent's own voice, the right next step, a finished article. Anything failing gets one targeted rewrite of just those sentences and is checked again; a wrong fact, place, fine print, misread number or invented story still failing is cut, anything else is flagged. The Verified report shows **Pass list (graded before saving)** with Pass / Fixed / Check per item. It's part of the same run, not a second version.
- Sales counts and "how buyers paid" only cover the days our Bright MLS records fully hold (e.g. "the 60 days ending Oct 19" instead of a 90-day window missing a month), and sale-to-list figures say how many homes sold above, at and below asking. Sources about another county are never cited. The payment example card says up front it's principal, interest and property tax only - homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance and condo/HOA fees aren't included. Its home price matches the article's home type: a single-family or move-up article uses the typical single-family house, a condo/townhome article the typical condo or townhome, anything else the lower-priced quarter of all homes - and its tax estimate comes from that same type's tax bills. Jumbo or not is judged per down payment against the home's county's own FHFA conforming loan limit (e.g. Loudoun County is higher than most of Pennsylvania); when a town spans counties with different limits, the card doesn't mark jumbo and the article says it depends on the amount borrowed and the county's limit. Above the limit it notes the rate is a conforming average and to get a jumbo quote. Articles never state flatly that a loan "is" jumbo.
- Every local government holding 1% or more of a town's homes must be named (e.g. a Baltimore address can be in Baltimore City, Baltimore County or Anne Arundel County); smaller verified places are grouped as "other towns", never left out as if they don't exist. Tiny slices are dropped only when they're bad map points far from the town's other homes. Place-specific and property-specific protections are added automatically: ground rent for Baltimore City/County, the federal lead-paint disclosure when the topic is older homes or most listings were built before 1978, and Maryland's disclaimer note (a seller who disclaims must still disclose known hidden health/safety defects). Every count that's a subset says which set it's from ("201 of the 203 townhomes that report a fee give a dollar amount").
- Property taxes appear as a dated median tax bill in dollars only - never as a percent of price (it reads like an official tax rate). Articles say taxes vary by municipality and to check the home's actual bill and assessment. Which office collects a closing tax is never stated (local procedure) - readers are told to ask the settlement company or attorney for an itemized estimate.
- Everything must fit the reader: programs, tax breaks and loan rules that require living in the home (first-time-buyer, homestead) are left out of rental and investment articles, and those articles get no payment example (investment loans need more down and price differently - readers are sent to a lender); rents stay general. Closing taxes and fees are named with who usually pays, never their rates - readers are told to get an itemized estimate. Towns and school districts are never presented as the complete list. The fact-check flags misses as **Doesn't fit this reader**.
- Manufactured homes are counted apart from one-level homes (land may be owned or leased); fee and "how buyers paid" figures always say how many listings or sales they come from.
- If the month's allowance is used: "You've used this month's AI allowance."
- After: **Pick its look** (tap a style on your real article), then **Finish your article** (optional): add a video (**Right after the intro** / **Just before the ending**), **End with a pitch** - "Readers finish with your form", **Show your work above it** (Recent Sales, My listings, Videos about the area - up to 6), and **End with my email signature** (IDX / No-MLS Premium). "Saved as a draft - nothing is published until you say so."
- Existing articles: **Draft with AI**, **Rewrite with AI** ("The AI writer will replace this article's current text with a new draft"), or **Finish with AI** for an unfinished one.

## The editor - 4 tabs
1. **Write** - title, subtitle and sections. **Add section** (or type "/"): *Text* - Heading, Subheading, Paragraph, Quote, Bulleted List, Numbered List, Callout Box, Divider; *Media* - Image, Image Gallery, YouTube Video; *Real Estate* - Property Card, Town Spotlight (from your IDX Areas towns), Agent Profile, QR Code; *Action* - Button, Call to Action, Contact Form (questions arrive in Leads), Lead Form (one of your forms), Related Articles, Disclaimer. Text tools: bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, link, text size. Fold, hide (and **Add back**) or **Delete for good** any section.
   - **Refine this section with AI**: **Rewrite it fresher**, **Expand with more depth**, **Make it shorter**, **Make it more local**, **Make it more personal**, **Simplify the language**, or type your own instruction. "Uses your monthly AI credits. Never adds new numbers."
2. **Look & cover** - **Add a cover photo** (a wide JPG; it leads the article, your Hub card and every share; JPG under 10MB), **Category** (Buyers, Sellers, Market Updates, Neighborhoods, Homeownership, Luxury, Investments, Open Houses, Local Guides, New Construction, Real Estate News), **Look** (the 5 looks), **Color** / **Finish**.
   - **AI cover**: the AI suggests a picture; describe or tweak it ("make it dusk, lose the snow") -> **Make the change** -> **Use as cover**. "Usually 20-40 seconds." Uses AI allowance; "Couldn't make that picture - nothing was charged."
3. **Fact-check & finish** - the **Verified** report: scores (Accuracy, Local detail, Usefulness, Sounds like you, Readability, Search, Compliance), **Still needs your eye** (flags such as Unsupported claim, Wrong number, Out-of-date number, Geography, Fair Housing, School wording, Your experience, Legal / tax, Unfinished text, Too technical for readers), **Numbers to double-check**, **Where the facts came from**, **Make it even stronger**.
4. **Review and Publish** - a checklist (**A real title**, **Cover photo**, **Category**, **Search description**, **Share image**) and every setting:
   - **Tags**, **Featured on Agent Hub** (pins it to the front of your Hub's articles).
   - **Article link** - the end of the address (/blogs/...). Changing it on a live article "breaks links people already shared and resets where it shows in Google" - **Change the link** / **Keep the old link**.
   - **On Google**: **Search title**, **Search description**, **Let Google find this article** (off = hidden from search, still works by link), with a Google preview.
   - **When it's shared**: **Share image** (otherwise the cover), **Share title**, **Share description**.
   - **Neighborhood pages** - link the towns to their pages on your IDX website.
   - **Where it shows**: **Show on my Agent Hub** (Blogs section), **Show on my IDX Site** (IDX: Journal and Latest Articles). "Hiding keeps it published - just off that page."
   - **End with my email signature** - your signature card replaces the small contact card.

## Publishing and scheduling
- **Publish now**, or **Or schedule it**: quick picks **Tomorrow 8 AM**, **This Saturday 9 AM**, **Next Monday 8 AM**, or your own date and time ("Goes live ...").
- Live articles: **Update published article**, **View live**, **Copy Link**. You can unpublish it (status Unpublished) and publish it again later.
- Autosaves (Saving... / Saved / Save failed). **Preview** any time.

## Can't do / common mistakes
- More than 5 towns or 4 sales in one AI article isn't possible.
- The AI never makes up statistics - give it your numbers in "Numbers you want cited".
- Town Spotlight needs towns set up in IDX > Areas.
- "Classic" is the standard look; "Luxe" is the premium one - don't mix them up.
- Basic, Premium and No-MLS plans can't show articles on an IDX site.
- Read the fact-check flags before publishing; you're responsible for what goes live.
- From the chat: "Publish my post", "schedule it for Tuesday at 9" or "show it on my Agent Hub" sets up a card - any fact-check flags and numbers to double-check show on the card before the button. "How many people read my post?" gives views per post (all time), and for one post its taps, leads and QR scans. Deleting a post stays on the Blogs page.
